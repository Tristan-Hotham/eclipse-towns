Data and code

Notes and sources

  • Eclipse geometry: Astronomy Engine by Don Cross (MIT licence), version 2.1.19, with its default Delta T model (the Espenak and Meeus formulas of the NASA Five Millennium Canon of Solar Eclipses) and my correction to the Moon's long-term acceleration.
  • Places: OS Open Names, Ordnance Survey, Open Government Licence v3.0 (contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2026); GeoNames cities1000 (CC BY 4.0) for Ireland, Northern Ireland, the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands. Coastline: Natural Earth (public domain).
  • History: English Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0): List of solar eclipses visible from the British Isles, Solar eclipse of August 2, 1133, Solar eclipse of April 8, 1652, Solar eclipse of May 3, 1715, Solar eclipse of May 22, 1724, Solar eclipse of June 29, 1927, Solar eclipse of August 11, 1999, Solar eclipse of September 23, 2090, Solar eclipse of August 21, 2017 (the 375-year figure) and ΔT (timekeeping).
  • Paper: Hotham, T. (2026) When did your town last see a total eclipse? Every path of totality over Britain and Ireland, AD 1000 to 2200. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23729635. Data and code: github.com/Tristan-Hotham/eclipse-towns (data CC BY 4.0, code MIT).
  • I wrote the text, with drafting and editing assistance from Claude (Anthropic), which also helped prepare the analysis code. I reviewed and checked all text and results.
